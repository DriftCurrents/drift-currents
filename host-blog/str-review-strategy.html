The STR Review Strategy: Stop Asking. Start Reminding.

August 2026 · 5 min read

← Back to Host Blog

Ask any group of STR hosts how they handle reviews and you will get a split room. Half send a checkout message asking for five stars. Half cringe at the idea. Both sides have a point.

Directly asking a guest to leave a review — especially one that specifies five stars — can feel transactional and desperate. It can also feel that way to the guest. A guest who felt nudged into a review is not in the same headspace as a guest who left one because they wanted to. We stopped asking. Here is what we do instead.

Why Five Stars Is the Only Acceptable Outcome

On Airbnb, a four-star review is not a good review. It is a signal to the algorithm and to future guests that something was off. Airbnb’s system treats anything below five stars as a negative data point. Three or more four-star reviews in a row will affect your search placement. A pattern of them will affect your Superhost status.

This is not about vanity. It is about visibility. A lower-ranked listing gets fewer impressions. Fewer impressions mean fewer bookings. A four-star average is a business problem dressed as a rating.

The Platform Review Window

Both Airbnb and VRBO have a review window after checkout — a set number of days during which the guest can leave a review. After that window closes, the review cannot be submitted. The review is most likely to be written when the stay is fresh: in the 24–48 hours after checkout when the guest is still thinking about the trip.

That window is when a physical reminder in the cabin pays off. A guest who saw the magnet on the fridge every morning of their stay already has it in their head. When checkout happens and the OTA sends its review prompt, the idea of leaving a review is not new to them.

The Magnet

We put a magnet on the refrigerator. It is a simple, branded reminder that reviews matter to us as hosts and that the platform will prompt them to leave one after checkout. It does not beg. It does not specify stars. It is just there — visible every morning when a guest gets coffee, every evening when they open the fridge.

By checkout, it has been seen a dozen times. When the OTA sends the review prompt, the guest already knows it is coming. They were ready before the email arrived.

Amazon Has Them Magnetic review reminder cards designed for short-term rentals are available on Amazon. Search for STR review reminder magnet. Put one on the refrigerator. Leave it there. That is the strategy.

Earn the Review During the Stay

No magnet earns a five-star review from a guest who had a problem that was not resolved. The reminder only works if the stay was already good. That means the mid-stay check-in message, the responsive communication when something comes up, and the property condition on arrival all have to hold up.

A guest who had a great stay and saw the magnet will leave a review. A guest who had a great stay and did not see the magnet might leave a review. A guest who had a problem that was not resolved will leave a review whether you ask or not — and it will not be five stars regardless of how nicely you asked.

Write Your Review of the Guest First

On Airbnb, reviews are revealed simultaneously after both parties submit or after the window closes. Writing your review of the guest first does not affect what they write about you. What it does is establish your documentation of the stay — positive or negative — before the window closes.

Write it within 24 hours of checkout while the details are fresh. Be honest. A glowing review of a difficult guest helps the next host who has to deal with them. An accurate negative review — specific, professional, factual — protects other hosts without violating platform guidelines.

When a Review Goes Wrong

Even with the best system, a bad review happens. How you respond matters more than you think.
