Why Your STR Cabin Needs a QR Code — And Where It Should Point

August 2026 · 4 min read

← Back to Host Blog

A guest sitting on your porch, coffee in hand, using the branded mug you gave them — that is the highest-value marketing moment you will ever have. They are relaxed, happy, and completely open to buying something connected to the experience they are having right now.

A QR code on the fridge or a framed poster in the cabin turns that moment into a click to your merch store. They buy the mug. They take it home. It sits on their desk at work. Their coworker asks about it. The coworker scans the QR code on the mug — the one that points to your booking site — and books a stay.

Two QR codes. Two jobs. One loop.

The Physical-to-Digital Bridge Where It Should Point Where to Place It UTM Tracking QR Codes on Merch

The Physical-to-Digital Bridge

Everything in the Stream layer — affiliate pages, merch stores, booking pages — lives online. Your guests are physically in your cabin. A QR code is what connects those two worlds.

Without it, the gap is too wide. A guest who loved the sheets might Google around for them later and find a different brand. A guest who wants to rebook might go to Airbnb and find a competitor first. A QR code placed intentionally, in the right spot, closes that gap while the guest is still engaged.

Where It Should Point

A QR code can point anywhere. The question is what you want the guest to do when they scan it.

  • Affiliate page — guests buy the items from the cabin, you earn a commission
  • Merch store — guests buy branded products, you earn the margin
  • Direct booking page — guests rebook without the OTA fee
  • Review page — guests leave a review while the stay is fresh

One QR code, one destination. Do not try to send a guest to a menu of options. Decide what action matters most at each placement location and point directly to it.

Where to Place It in the Cabin

Placement determines scan rate. A QR code buried in the welcome book gets scanned once. A QR code on the refrigerator or in a framed print on the wall gets scanned by guests who have been staring at it all weekend.

In-cabin QR codes — on the fridge, in a framed poster, on the coffee station — should point to your merch store. The guest is in the moment. They love the mug in their hand. They want one to take home. Give them the path directly there. A QR code by the checkout reminder can point to your review page. Each placement has one job — pick the job that fits the moment.

The mug itself carries a different QR code entirely. That one points to your booking site. The mug leaves with the guest. The QR goes with it. The person who scans it from a coworker’s desk has never heard of your property — they need to land somewhere that tells them where you are, what the experience is, and how to book. Your property site does that. Your store does not.

UTM Tracking: Know What Is Working

A plain QR code tells you nothing. A QR code with UTM parameters tells you exactly how many guests scanned it, which placement drove the most traffic, and whether those scans turned into purchases or bookings.

UTM parameters are tags added to the URL your QR code points to. They are invisible to the guest but tracked by your analytics platform. With them, you know which QR code in which room drove what result. Without them, you are guessing.

Tracking Is Not Optional If you cannot measure which QR code is driving results, you cannot improve your placement, your destination, or your copy. UTM tracking is what separates a QR code strategy from a QR code decoration.

The QR on the Mug Goes to Your Booking Site

The in-cabin QR codes point guests to your store. The QR code printed on the mug itself serves a completely different purpose and points somewhere completely different: your property website or booking page.

Here is why. The person who eventually scans that mug is not the guest who stayed. It is their coworker. Their friend. Their family member. Someone who has never heard of your property, who sees a branded mug on someone’s desk and scans the code out of curiosity. That person needs context — where is this place, what is it like, how do I book it. Your store does not answer those questions. Your site does.

The mug is a free gift. You give it to the guest at checkout or leave it waiting for them. They take it home. It sits on their desk. It becomes a daily advertisement for your property in an office full of people who take weekend trips. Every scan from that mug is a warm lead from a trusted referral source — someone who already knows a person who stayed and loved it.
