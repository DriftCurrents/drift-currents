What a Merch Store Does for Your STR Brand (And Why Most Hosts Never Build One)

August 2026 · 5 min read

← Back to Host Blog

Your guests stayed. They loved it. They went home. That is where most hosts lose them forever. A merch store changes that.

A branded mug on a guest’s desk is your property name on their desk. Every time they use it, they think about the trip. Every time someone asks where they got it, they tell the story. That is marketing you did not have to pay for, delivered by a guest who already loves your property.

What a Merch Store Is What It Does Products That Work The QR Bridge What It Takes

What a Merch Store Is

A merch store is an online storefront selling branded physical products tied to your STR property. Mugs, tumblers, coasters, t-shirts, hats — items a guest might buy as a souvenir, a gift, or because they genuinely loved their stay and want something to remember it by.

The products are printed on demand. Nothing is manufactured until someone orders. You do not hold inventory. You do not ship anything. An order comes in, it goes to a fulfillment partner, and it ships directly to the customer. Your job is to have a store. Someone else handles the rest.

What It Does for Your Brand

Most STR hosts think of their property as a place to rent. A merch store turns it into a brand. That is a different thing. A brand has a name, a look, and a presence that extends beyond the four walls of the cabin. Guests carry it with them. They share it. They come back to it.

A merch store also creates a second revenue stream that requires nothing from you after setup. A guest buys a tumbler three months after their stay. You earn the margin. No new booking required.

The Marketing Math A guest who buys a branded mug and uses it at work is showing your property name to every person who walks past their desk. That is a marketing impression you did not pay for. Multiply that by every guest who buys something and you have a distributed marketing channel running on autopilot.

Products That Work

Not all merch works equally well for STR brands. The products that work are the ones guests use in front of other people.

  • Mugs — used daily, visible at desks and in kitchens, high repeat exposure
  • Tumblers — taken everywhere, visible outside the home, conversation starters
  • Coasters — stay in the home, visible to guests in the buyer’s home
  • Apparel — the highest visibility item, but only if the design is something people actually want to wear

Generic designs do not move. Designs that capture something specific about your property — the river, the mountain, the vibe — sell because they mean something to the guest who stayed there.

The QR Bridge: From the Cabin to the Store

The gap most hosts cannot close is getting guests to the store after they leave. The answer is a QR code inside the cabin — on a card, on a coaster, on the welcome book — that points directly to your merch store. A guest who sees it during their stay, while they are most engaged with your property, is the most likely buyer.

That QR code is also trackable. You know exactly how many guests scanned it, when, and whether they purchased. That data tells you whether the store is working and where to put the QR code to maximize scans.

The mug itself carries a different QR code with a different job. That one points to your booking site, not your store. When a guest takes the mug to work and a coworker scans it, they are not looking to buy a mug — they are looking at a property they have never heard of and deciding whether to book it. Your site answers that question. The in-cabin QR drives the sale. The mug QR drives the next booking.

What It Takes to Build One

A merch store requires design, platform setup, product selection, fulfillment configuration, and a QR code strategy. None of it is complicated individually. Together, it is several hours of focused work to do right — and ongoing attention to keep running.

Most hosts who try to build one themselves get partway through and stall on the design. The store never launches. The guests who would have bought something never get the chance.
