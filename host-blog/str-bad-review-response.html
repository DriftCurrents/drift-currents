How to Respond to a Bad STR Review

August 2026 · 6 min read

← Back to Host Blog

A bad review feels personal. It is not. It is a data point on a public page that future guests will read before they decide whether to book. Your response to it is the thing you actually control — and it matters more than the review itself.

The guest who left the review has already moved on. Your response is not for them. It is for every future guest reading your listing who wants to know what kind of host you are when something goes wrong.

Wait Before You Respond Who the Response Is For What to Say What Not to Say The One Rule When to Flag for Removal

Wait Before You Respond

Do not respond immediately. You will write something you regret. Read the review, close the app, and come back to it in 24 hours. The review is not going anywhere. Your response, once submitted, cannot be edited or deleted. Write it when you are calm enough to write something a future guest would respect.

Who the Response Is For

Say it again before you type a single word: this response is not for the guest who wrote the review. It is for future guests. A future guest reading a bad review wants to know two things: is this a pattern, and how does the host handle it when something goes wrong?

A host who responds professionally, acknowledges the concern, and explains their process communicates competence. A host who argues, makes excuses, or attacks the guest communicates the opposite — and confirms the impression the bad review left.

What to Say

Keep it short. Three to four sentences is enough. Acknowledge that the guest’s experience did not meet expectations, explain briefly what you have done or would do differently, and close with something that signals you take hosting seriously.

Example Response — Legitimate Complaint “We appreciate [Guest] taking the time to share their experience. The [specific issue] they encountered was not the standard we hold ourselves to, and we have addressed it directly. We take all feedback seriously and are committed to providing every guest with a property that is clean, well-maintained, and exactly as described. We hope to have the opportunity to host them again under better circumstances.”
Example Response — House Rule Violation Involved “We appreciate [Guest] sharing their perspective. Our house rules exist to protect the property and ensure a consistent experience for all guests, and we apply them fairly and consistently. We wish [Guest] well and appreciate their stay.”

What Not to Say

  • Do not argue specific facts publicly — “That is not true” or “The guest is lying” reads as defensive and unprofessional regardless of whether you are right
  • Do not list everything the guest did wrong — a future guest does not want to read a dispute, they want to know you are a reasonable host
  • Do not be sarcastic or passive-aggressive — it will read worse than the original review
  • Do not over-apologize for things that were not your fault — it implies guilt
  • Do not mention the guest’s name in a way that sounds like a callout

The One Rule

Never argue facts in a public response. Even if the guest is factually wrong about something specific — the cabin was clean, the check-in instructions were clear, there was no spider — a public rebuttal makes you look like you are prioritizing being right over being a good host. Save the factual record for a platform support case if one is warranted. Keep the public response professional.

The Audience Is Not the Reviewer Every word of your public response is read by future guests, not the guest who wrote the review. Write for the audience that has not booked yet. They are deciding whether you are the kind of host they want to deal with.

When to Flag for Removal

Airbnb will remove reviews that violate their content policy. Reviews that contain false factual claims, discriminatory language, threats, or content from a guest who did not actually stay at the property are eligible for removal. Reviews that are simply negative or unfair are not.

Flag a review through the Airbnb resolution center with specific documentation of the policy violation. A vague flag — “this review is unfair” — will not result in removal. A specific flag — “this review contains a factually false claim about [X], and here is my documentation” — has a better chance.

Do not count on removal. Respond professionally, flag if there are grounds, and move on. The best antidote to one bad review is a string of five-star reviews on either side of it.

Build the System That Prevents Bad Reviews

The best response to a bad review is fewer bad reviews. The screening process, turnover documentation, and mid-stay communication are what get you there.
