Timestamped Photos Before Every STR Check-In — Always Landscape

August 2026 · 5 min read

← Back to Host Blog

A guest checks out. You walk the property. Something is broken. Is it from this guest or the one before? You are not sure. The guest is not answering. You have no proof either way.

Timestamped photos taken after every clean, before every check-in, close that gap completely. They are your before state, documented, dated, and irrefutable. Every host should be doing this from day one. Most do not start until after the first dispute.

Always Landscape Two-Minute Setup What to Shoot When to Shoot The Bonus: Listing Photos The Airbnb Text Rule

Always Landscape. No Exceptions.

Turn your phone sideways before you take a single photo. Every photo in your documentation library should be landscape orientation. This is not optional.

Here is why it matters: damage claim photos submitted to Airbnb, photos used in a dispute, photos you share with an insurance company — all of them display better and communicate more information in landscape. A portrait photo of a room shows you a slice of it. A landscape photo shows you the room.

More importantly: every OTA listing photo is displayed in landscape format. If you ever want to use a turnover photo in your listing — and you will — it has to be landscape. Shooting portrait is shooting content you cannot use for two purposes.

Portrait Kills the Photo A portrait photo submitted as a damage claim or listing photo is immediately less useful than a landscape one. The habit costs nothing to build. Turn the phone sideways. Every time.

Two-Minute Setup

Go into your phone’s camera settings and enable the date and time watermark. On most phones this is in the camera settings under watermark or timestamp. It adds the date and time directly to every photo you take. Once it is on, you do not have to think about it again.

The timestamp on the photo is metadata. The watermark on the image is visible proof. You want both.

What to Shoot

Cover the entire property systematically after every clean:

  • Every room from the doorway — the establishing shot that shows the full room condition
  • Every bed, made and staged
  • Kitchen counters, stovetop, sink
  • Bathrooms — toilet, tub/shower, vanity
  • Any surface or item that has been damaged before
  • Outdoor spaces: deck, firepit, any furniture
  • Smart locks, cameras, and any equipment visible to guests

You are not trying to build an art portfolio. You are building a timestamped record that every surface was in good condition immediately before this guest arrived.

When to Shoot

After the clean is complete and before the next guest has access. The cleaner finishes, you walk the property and shoot. Or your cleaner shoots for you on the same timeline. The photos need to be taken in the window between the clean and check-in — that is the documentation that matters.

Photos taken the day before or the morning of when the clean happened yesterday do not establish the same before state. Same day, same window, every time.

The Bonus: Listing Photos

A property shot after a professional clean with good natural light is a property shot at its best. Those turnover photos are often better than the original listing photos — the staging is consistent, the light is real, and the property looks exactly as a guest will find it.

Keep a folder of your best turnover shots. When your listing photos start to look dated or stale, you already have replacements. The documentation habit produces listing assets as a byproduct.

The Airbnb Text Rule: Scrape the Timestamp Before You Upload

Airbnb does not allow text overlays on listing photos. The date and time watermark on your turnover photos violates this rule. Before you upload any turnover photo to your Airbnb listing, remove the timestamp.

This is a separate step from your documentation library. Keep the original timestamped photo for your records. Create a copy with the timestamp removed for the listing. Two files, two purposes.

There is more to know about Airbnb’s photo rules — text on pictures is a topic that affects more than just timestamps. We cover it in a dedicated post: Know Your OTA: Text on Listing Photos (coming soon).
