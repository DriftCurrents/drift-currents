The STR Turnover: Your Cleaner Relationship Is Your Rating

August 2026 · 6 min read

← Back to Host Blog

The single most important operational relationship you have as an STR host is with your cleaner. Not your OTA. Not your guests. Your cleaner.

Guests rate cleanliness separately from everything else. It is the first thing they notice when they walk in and the last thing they think about when they leave a review. A great stay with a dirty cabin gets three stars. A mediocre stay in a spotless cabin gets five. Your cleaner determines your rating more than you do.

The Relationship First

A good cleaner for a short-term rental is not easy to find and not easy to replace. STR turnovers are harder than standard cleaning jobs — tighter timelines, higher standards, unpredictable guest damage, and the pressure of an incoming guest who expects perfection. The people who do this well know their value.

Treat this relationship accordingly. Pay fairly. Communicate clearly. Give them everything they need to succeed — well-stocked supplies, a property that is easy to clean, and respect for their time and expertise. A cleaner who feels valued stays. A cleaner who does not finds another host.

Losing a good cleaner between bookings is one of the worst operational situations a host can face. The cost is not just one bad turnover. It is the scramble to find someone, the learning curve of training them, and every review that gets written before the new person gets it right.

The Checklist Question

Many STR host resources recommend giving your cleaner a detailed turnover checklist. It is not bad advice. A checklist ensures consistency and creates a record that every item was addressed. We get it.

We do not use one. Here is why: handing someone a clipboard and saying “check these boxes” is a signal, whether you intend it that way or not. It can read as distrust. It can feel like micromanagement to someone who has been doing this professionally for years. And it can damage a relationship that took months to build.

That does not mean you should not have one. It means you should ask. A simple conversation — “Would a checklist be helpful, or do you prefer to work your own system?” — respects their professionalism and lets them tell you what works for them. Some cleaners want one. Some do not. Either answer is fine. The question is what matters.

Ask, Don’t Impose Before you hand your cleaner a checklist, ask if they want one. Frame it as a tool for them, not a report card for you. “I put this together in case it’s helpful — let me know if you’d rather work your own way.” That conversation preserves the relationship and still gets you what you need.

What You Control: The Property Setup

The easiest turnover is one where the property makes the cleaner’s job as straightforward as possible. That starts with how the property is designed and stocked — decisions that are yours, not your cleaner’s.

  • Minimal decorative items mean fewer surfaces to dust and fewer things to move
  • Simple bedding without decorative pillows or shams cleans faster and looks consistently good
  • Consistent product placement — same supplies in the same spots every time — speeds up restocking
  • A property that is easy to clean gets cleaned well in less time

Every complexity you add to the property is a complexity you add to the turnover. Knick knacks, layered bedding, specialty finishes, and hard-to-reach surfaces cost your cleaner time and cost you money or quality.

The Locked Supply Cabinet

Stock the property with everything your cleaner needs to restock between every stay: paper towels, toilet paper, trash bags, dish soap, laundry pods, cleaning supplies, backup amenities, light bulbs. Everything in one place, locked against guest access, refilled by you between stays or on a scheduled delivery.

A cleaner who has to improvise because a supply ran out is a cleaner who cannot do their best work. A cleaner who walks into a fully stocked cabinet can focus on cleaning, not logistics.

Your Role in the Turnover

Your cleaner cleans. Your job in the turnover is documentation: timestamped photos of the property after every clean, before the next guest arrives. Every room, every surface, every known damage point.

Those photos are your record. If a guest claims something was broken before they arrived, your photos say otherwise. If something was damaged during a stay, your photos establish the before state. Without them, you are working from memory in a dispute where the guest has a platform and you have nothing.

See our full post on timestamped photos and how to do them right.

The Full Stay System

The turnover is one piece. Automated messaging, photo documentation, and review strategy complete the picture.
