Best STR Host Supplies for Mountain Cabins — What We Actually Stock

August 2026 · 8 min read

← Back to Host Blog

We’ve hosted hundreds of guests across two dog-friendly cabins with riverfront and river access on the Toccoa River in Suches, Georgia. In that time, we’ve learned one thing clearly: the supplies you choose shape the reviews you get.

A scratchy pillow gets mentioned. A kitchen without measuring cups gets mentioned. A great shampoo gets mentioned. Cabin supplies aren’t invisible to guests — the good ones get noticed and the bad ones definitely do.

This is exactly what we stock, why we chose it, and where to get it.

Bedding & Linens: Where Reviews Are Won and Lost

Nothing tanks a five-star stay faster than poor sleep. We’ve tested several brands and landed on these:

  • California Design Den 400TC Cotton Sateen Sheets — soft, machine washable, holds up over repeated laundering. The 6-piece king set includes four pillowcases, which matters for turnover efficiency.
  • AllerEase Mattress Protectors — guests don’t see them but they protect your investment. Maximum allergy protection and machine washable.
  • AllerEase Pillow Protectors (4-pack) — moisture wicking with zippered closure. Stock more than you think you need.
  • TasticTotes 40L Clear Storage Bags — we zip-tie extra linens inside these and leave them in the closet. Guests see organized bags and it signals intentionality. Bonus: bug protection between stays.
Host Tip Only set out linens for your base occupancy. Extras go in labeled storage bags. This reduces waste, signals cleanliness, and gives guests the sense that everything has been thought of.

Kitchen: Stock It Like You Live There

Guests cooking at the cabin is a feature, not a nuisance. Make it easy for them.

  • Corelle Vitrelle 18-Piece Dinnerware Set — chip resistant, lightweight, and dishwasher safe. Looks clean even after hundreds of uses.
  • Mikasa Oliver 65-Piece Flatware Set — hammered satin finish looks upscale. Guests notice nice flatware and it costs you almost nothing extra.
  • Pyrex 3-Pack Measuring Cups — the most-borrowed kitchen item that hosts forget. Every guest who tries to cook will look for these.
  • Royal Craft Bamboo Cutting Boards — doubles as a charcuterie board for the wine-and-cheese crowd. Mountain cabin guests skew toward that.
  • Ziploc Variety Pack (190ct) — guests use these for leftovers, trail snacks, and bait storage. Stock them.
  • Zeppoli Cotton Dish Towels (15-pack) — buy in bulk, wash easily, blue color hides stains better than white.
  • Reynolds Wrap Aluminum Foil — guests always need it. Foil packets on the grill are a mountain cabin staple.

Safety: The Non-Negotiables

Airbnb requires hosts to provide specific safety equipment. Do not skip this category.

  • First Alert SMCO410 Smoke & CO Combo Detector — We searched for three years to find a smart smoke detector with a 10-year sealed lithium battery that integrated with Ring. Nobody made one until 2025. Our second guest after installation burned a steak in the kitchen and Ring called us and sent a notification simultaneously. We pay extra for landlines in our cabins — we called the guests directly on the cabin landline to confirm everyone was OK and it was just a burned steak, while on the phone with Ring on our second phone canceling the fire department before they arrived. No false alarm charge. No panicked guests. These work. Do not settle for a standalone detector that nobody monitors.
  • BAND-AID Portable First Aid Kit — Airbnb requires one. Keep it in a locked supply cabinet. A guest stole ours when we left it out. Put a note in your house manual telling guests it is available upon request.
  • Prepared Hero Fire Blankets — mount one near the stove and one near the grill. In a real fire, who actually remembers how to operate an extinguisher under pressure? Everyone knows how to throw a blanket. Keep the extinguisher too — it is required — but the blanket is the one a guest will actually use correctly in an emergency.
  • Kidde Fire Extinguisher — required in most jurisdictions. Check yours.
Host Tip Keep the first aid kit in a locked supply cabinet, not out in the open. Note its availability in your house manual. A guest stole ours when it was visible. Learn from that before you have to.

Leak Protection: The One Thing Hosts Always Regret Skipping

Remote properties and water leaks are a nightmare combination. A burst pipe discovered by a guest — or not discovered for days between stays — can cost tens of thousands of dollars in water damage. This is the single category most hosts skip and most regret.

  • Moen Flo Automatic Water Shutoff (3/4in) — monitors flow, pressure, moisture, and temperature. Automatically shuts off the water supply if a leak is detected. WiFi app control from anywhere in the world.
  • Moen Flo Smart Leak Detectors (3-pack) — place under sinks and near appliances. Pair with the shutoff for complete coverage. App alerts the moment moisture is detected.

Our Moen Flo system notified us at 7 AM on a morning when the cabin was vacant. We turned off the water supply from our phone before we even got out of bed. When the plumber arrived, he told us we had prevented a catastrophe. A neighbor who had walked by said it was pouring like a waterfall. Without the Moen Flo, we would have found out days later when the next guest arrived.

If you own a remote STR property, the Moen Flo system is the single highest-ROI purchase on this entire list.

Smart Home: Run Your Cabin From Your Couch

The right smart home setup lets you manage your property remotely without relying on neighbors or a local co-host for every small issue.

  • Schlage Encode Smart Deadbolt — keyless entry with WiFi app control. Generate unique guest codes per booking and expire them automatically at checkout. Compatible with Alexa, Google, and Ring. One critical note: the Schlage Encode is sometimes marketed as Airbnb compatible, meaning you can grant Airbnb permission to access and manage your lock. Do not do this. Your lock controls who enters your property — keep that control with you, not with a platform. Still keep a lockbox on the property for when the batteries die or a guest cannot figure out the keypad.
  • TP-Link Deco X55 WiFi 6 Mesh System — covers up to 6,500 sq ft and lets you create multiple networks. We run a separate admin network for security cameras, HVAC, and other smart home devices, and a guest network for guest use only. Plug your Deco into a Kasa smart plug so you can remotely reboot it when guests call about WiFi issues, without driving to the cabin. Guests with bad WiFi leave bad reviews. This is not optional.
  • Kasa Matter Smart Plug (4-pack) — connect your mesh router to one. Remotely reboot it when guests have WiFi issues without driving to the cabin.
  • Ecobee Smart Thermostat Premium — gives you full remote control and lets you set maximum heat and minimum cool limits. The first thing we do is disable the energy-saving features. We do not respond well to auto-adjusting thermostats and will not ask our guests to either. What matters is setting guardrails: no guest is turning your cabin into a sauna by cranking heat to 80, and no guest is turning it into an igloo by setting AC to 50 and destroying your system. Set your limits and let guests control within them.
  • Ring Cameras — cover every entrance with two cameras: one solar and one plug-in, mounted from different directions covering the same entry point. The plug-in does not record when power is lost. The solar records during a power outage, and if your internet equipment is on a UPS and still running, you can watch those recordings in real time. Link them so when one detects motion, both record. We have multiple Ring camera models across our properties. The goal is no blind spots and no single point of failure at any entrance. We recently had guests attempt to turn off every single camera on the property. We caught the whole thing because each camera points at another camera. They could not get them all.
The smart lock and camera setup above is only half the equation. The other half is knowing who you let in before they arrive. Read our full guide on how to screen STR guests and protect your property →

Guest Amenities: The Bathroom Details That Show Up in Reviews

Guests notice the bathroom. Not always consciously, but when something is good, they mention it. When something is bad or missing, they mention that too. The difference between a forgettable bathroom and one guests comment on is not expensive. It is intentional.

We stopped using single-use bottles years ago. They are wasteful, expensive at scale, and make the shower look like a hotel that is trying too hard. Instead, we use a set of three refillable 16.9oz pump dispensers mounted in the shower, filled from gallon jugs. One refill per month instead of restocking bottles every single turnover.

  • Ginger Lily Farms Moisturizing Shampoo — Fragrance-Free, 1 Gallon — fragrance-free and gentle for all hair types. Guests with color-treated hair, curly hair, and sensitive skin all handle it well. The gallon jug fills the shower dispenser multiple times per stay.
  • Ginger Lily Farms Moisturizing Conditioner — Fragrance-Free, 1 Gallon — pairs with the shampoo. Guests who ask what we use almost always say their hair felt better than usual. Buy the gallon jug, not the retail size.
  • Ginger Lily Farms Nourishing Body Wash — Fragrance-Free, 1 Gallon — completes the trio. Same brand, same dispenser, cohesive look in the shower.
  • Shampoo and Conditioner Dispenser Set — Refillable, Clear, Set of 3, 16.9oz, with Waterproof Labels — fill from the gallon jugs and mount in the shower. Waterproof labels stay legible. Clean look guests appreciate. This is what we put the Ginger Lily Farms products in.
Host Tip Label each dispenser clearly. Guests in the shower should not have to guess which bottle is shampoo and which is conditioner. Waterproof labels solve this. The set comes with them.

Dog-Friendly Essentials: The Details Guests Actually Rave About

If you welcome dogs, go all the way. We welcome dogs and are often booked when our competitors are not. Half-hearted dog amenities don’t earn mentions. Intentional ones do — and they show up in reviews more than almost anything else.

  • Gorilla Grip Dog Bowl Set with Silicone Mat — stainless steel, BPA-free, slip-resistant mat with high-edge spill containment. Dishwasher safe.
  • Milk-Bone MaroSnacks — leave a welcome treat out for dog guests. Gets mentioned in reviews more than you’d think. Costs almost nothing.
  • Bounce Pet Hair Dryer Sheets (180-count) — essential for getting fur off linens between stays. 3X pet hair fighters.
  • Earth Rated Dog Poop Bags (120-count) — stock these near the door. Guests forget their bags constantly, especially for cabin trips. Small but noticeable touch.
Host Tip Dog-friendly doesn’t have to mean anything goes. A dog bowl, a treat canister, and a bag dispenser by the door costs you under $40 and turns a “pet-friendly” listing into a “they actually thought about our dog” review.
