How to Handle a Problem Guest on Airbnb

August 2026 · 7 min read

← Back to Host Blog

The best problem guest situation is the one you prevented. The second best is the one you handled correctly from the first sign of trouble. This post is about both — and what to do when prevention did not work and the guest is already in your property.

This process is specific to Airbnb. VRBO handles escalations differently. Know which platform you are dealing with before you start the clock.

Document First, Always Keep Everything On-Platform The On-Platform Warning When to Contact Airbnb When to Cancel After the Stay

Document First, Always

The moment you suspect a problem, start documenting. Screenshot everything. Camera footage timestamps. Messages on the platform. If something is happening at the property — extra guests, noise, damage visible on camera — capture it with time and date attached before you do anything else.

Documentation is not evidence of a problem. It is the foundation of your case if the problem escalates. A host who documented everything has options. A host who did not has a story and nothing else.

Your timestamped pre-check-in photos become part of this documentation. The before state is already established. You are now building the during and after.

Keep Everything On-Platform

Every communication with a problem guest must happen through the Airbnb message thread. Do not call them. Do not text them. Do not move the conversation anywhere outside the platform.

On-platform messages are timestamped, logged, and available to Airbnb support if you need to escalate. Off-platform communication disappears the moment the guest decides to deny it happened. Keep the record on the platform where it cannot be disputed.

The On-Platform Warning

If a guest is violating house rules — extra guests, smoking, unauthorized parties — address it through the message thread first. State the violation clearly, reference the specific house rule, and give them the opportunity to correct it. Keep the tone professional and factual.

This serves two purposes. First, it gives the guest a chance to comply, which ends the problem without escalation. Second, it creates a documented record that you identified the violation, communicated it to the guest, and gave them a reasonable opportunity to address it. That record matters if you need Airbnb to step in or if the guest later disputes what happened.

Do Not Threaten. State Facts. “Our house rules allow a maximum of 4 guests. Our cameras show more than 4 people on the property. Please comply with the occupancy limit immediately or we will need to contact Airbnb support to resolve this.” That is a fact statement. Not a threat. Keep it that way.

When to Contact Airbnb

Contact Airbnb support when the guest does not comply after an on-platform warning, when there is damage that will require a claim, when you feel unsafe, or when the situation has escalated beyond what you can resolve directly.

When you contact Airbnb, have your documentation ready: the specific message thread showing the violation and your communication, camera footage if applicable, and your house rules as listed on the platform. The clearer and more organized your documentation, the faster and more favorably Airbnb can act.

Airbnb support quality varies. Some cases are resolved quickly. Some require persistence and escalation within their system. Document every support interaction including the case number, the agent name if provided, and what was said.

When to Cancel

Cancellation is a last resort, not a first response. A cancelled reservation creates friction for both sides and Airbnb does not make it easy. But there are situations where it is the right call: a guest who has violated house rules and refused to comply, a guest who has damaged the property during the stay, or a guest whose behavior creates a safety concern.

On Airbnb, a host-initiated cancellation for guest house rule violations can be penalty-free if the violation is documented and reported correctly through support. Do not cancel unilaterally and then report. Contact Airbnb first, document the violation, and let support guide the cancellation process. The sequence matters.

Never Cancel Without Airbnb Involvement A host-initiated cancellation without documented support involvement can result in penalties, refund requirements, and a mark against your hosting record. If you believe cancellation is warranted, contact Airbnb before you cancel and document the conversation.

After the Stay: The Review and the Claim

Write your review of the guest within 24 hours of checkout. Be accurate. Be specific. Be professional. A vague negative review protects no one. A specific, factual review — “Guest arrived with more people than declared, violated occupancy rules after being notified, and left the property in condition requiring professional remediation” — protects every host who looks this guest up before accepting their next booking.

If there is damage, file your claim through Airbnb’s AirCover process within the required window. Your timestamped before photos, camera documentation, and on-platform communication are your evidence. Submit everything. Follow up persistently.

Before any of this happens, the best outcome is a guest who was screened carefully enough before booking that the problem never started. See our post on how to dissuade bad guests from booking for the full prevention system.

When a Review Comes In Anyway

Even a well-handled situation sometimes produces a bad review. How you respond is what future guests actually read.
