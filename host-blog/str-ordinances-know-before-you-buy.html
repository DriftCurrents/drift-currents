I Bought Three Different Smoke Detectors for One Property. Here Is Why.

STR ordinances dictate what you can install before a guest ever arrives. Get familiar with them before you buy anything.

Three smoke detectors. One property. Same rooms. Different detectors each time, not because we wanted to upgrade, but because we had to.

When we set up our first short-term rental in Fannin County, Georgia, we researched what was required and bought what the county said we needed. At the time, AA battery smoke detectors were compliant. We bought the Ring Alarm Smoke and CO Detector because it connected to our Ring system and sent alerts to our phones. Smart, connected, exactly what you want when you are managing a rental remotely.

Four months later, Fannin County changed the ordinance. New requirement: 10-year sealed lithium battery smoke detectors. The AA battery detectors we had just installed were no longer compliant. We pulled them out and bought a second set. Then we found a detector we actually liked better for STR use and switched again.

Three smoke detectors. One property. All because we did not anticipate that the ordinance would change, and because we did not know enough about local STR regulations to see it coming.

Why This Matters More Than Most New Hosts Think

Most new STR hosts focus on the listing, the photos, the pricing. The compliance side feels like paperwork. It is not paperwork. It is a list of things you are legally required to have installed before your first guest checks in, and those requirements directly determine what products you buy.

Buy the wrong smoke detector and you are not just wasting money. You are potentially operating out of compliance. If something goes wrong during a guest stay and your property is not up to local code, you have a serious problem that goes well beyond a bad review.

The relationship between local ordinances and product selection is direct and often overlooked. Know the ordinance first. Buy the product second.

What STR Ordinances Typically Cover

Every jurisdiction is different, but here is what local STR ordinances most commonly address. Each one affects what you buy or install.

Smoke detectors. Battery type (AA vs. sealed lithium), placement (inside every bedroom, outside sleeping areas, on every level), interconnection requirements (when one sounds, all sound). Sealed 10-year lithium is increasingly the standard. If your county requires it, that rules out most smart smoke detectors, which run on AA or 9-volt batteries.

Carbon monoxide detectors. Required near sleeping areas in any property with gas appliances, attached garage, or fuel-burning heating systems. Placement and battery requirements often mirror smoke detector rules.

Fire extinguishers. Type (ABC rated), size, and placement. Some counties require one per floor. Some require them mounted, not just stored in a cabinet.

Occupancy limits. Maximum number of guests per bedroom or per square foot. This affects how you write your listing and what you allow in your house rules.

Parking. Number of vehicles allowed on the property. Relevant if you have neighbors and a limited driveway.

Noise and quiet hours. Some counties define quiet hours that you are responsible for enforcing. Put them in your house manual.

Permits and registration. Many counties require an STR permit or business license before you can legally operate. Some require annual renewal. Some require a physical inspection of the property.

Pool and hot tub safety. Fencing requirements, self-closing gate latches, drain covers, and in some jurisdictions, required signage about no diving or no lifeguard on duty.

How to Find Your Local STR Ordinances

Start with your county or municipality website. Search for "short-term rental ordinance" or "vacation rental regulations" along with your county name. If you cannot find it online, call the county planning or zoning department directly. Ask specifically:

  • Do I need a permit or license to operate an STR?
  • What smoke and CO detector type and placement is required?
  • Are there occupancy limits?
  • Are there any fire safety inspections required before I list?
  • How do I stay notified if the ordinance changes?

That last question matters. County websites do not email you when regulations change. You have to stay plugged in. Some counties have email lists for business license holders. Sign up for everything available.

What to Do When the Ordinance Changes

It will change. Local governments are still catching up to the STR industry, and ordinances in popular vacation rental markets are actively evolving. What was compliant when you opened may not be compliant two years later.

Build a habit of checking your county's STR rules annually. Set a calendar reminder. If you are in a county with an active STR community, join local host groups where news about ordinance changes travels fast. In Fannin County, we heard about the smoke detector change from another host before we heard it from the county.

When something changes, move on it quickly. Inspectors in STR-heavy markets are increasingly active, and operating out of compliance is not a risk worth taking.

The Smoke Detector We Use Now

After three rounds of smoke detectors, we landed on sealed 10-year lithium units that meet Fannin County's current requirements. They are not connected to our phone the way the Ring detector was, but they meet code, they do not need battery replacements, and we never worry about a guest checking out and leaving a chirping low-battery detector for the next guest to deal with.

If your county allows AA battery smoke detectors and you want phone notifications, the Ring Alarm Smoke and CO Detector is genuinely excellent for remote property management. Know your local requirement before you buy it.

The short version: Look up your county STR ordinance before you order a single piece of safety equipment. The ordinance tells you what battery type, what placement, and what certifications are required. Buy to the spec, not to the product listing.

The Bigger Picture

Smoke detectors are the most visible example, but the principle applies to everything. Lock type, camera placement, pool fencing, occupancy limits. Every category of property setup has a local ordinance layer that may constrain or dictate your product choices.

We built our property setup process around this sequence: research the ordinance, then build the product list, then buy, then install. In that order. Not the other way around.

Three smoke detectors taught us that.
